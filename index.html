THE METHOD

Solve it, search it, or step it.

A coupon bond’s price is just its cash flows discounted back to today. Stack ten bonds and that becomes a matrix equation — and once it is a matrix equation, there is more than one honest way to invert it.

PRICING IDENTITY

C · d = P

MatrixDirect linear solve of the cash-flow system
SolverMinimise squared pricing error
IterativeForward substitution, one maturity at a time

What it showed.

Three results worth keeping from the original run.

01

The prices come back

Multiplying the cash-flow matrix back through the bootstrapped discount factors reproduces all ten market prices exactly. That round-trip is the check that the curve is right.

Verification
02

All three agree

The linear solve, the global optimiser and the hand-rolled iteration return the same discount factors to four decimals — three different routes, one curve.

Consistency
03

Forwards fall faster

Spot and YTM peak near 5.14% at two years, then drift down to about 3.9%. The forward curve starts higher at 5.21% and drops to 2.08% — the marginal rates that the spot curve averages over.

Shape

And five tickers.

The second half of the project leaves rates behind: daily closes and volumes from January 2012, cumulative returns, a correlation matrix, and a CSV round-trip.

Prices were pulled through pandas_datareader, written out one CSV per ticker, then read back and recombined into a single frame — the round-trip being the point of the exercise rather than the returns themselves.
